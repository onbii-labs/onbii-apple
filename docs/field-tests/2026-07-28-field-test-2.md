# Field Test 2 — 28 July 2026

A second walk, one day later, on the builds
[Milestone 1.6](../milestones/milestone-1.6.md) produced. Its job was to answer
one question — *does a Watch recording survive a walk with the Workout app
running* — and to produce fresh evidence for the recognition thread.

It answered the first question, and the answer is better than expected. It also
produced three findings that had nowhere to surface yesterday, because yesterday
the capture failed before anything downstream could be observed.

## Conditions

A morning walk leaving from home in Breda. Recording started on Apple Watch
Ultra 2 at the front door and ran until the app stopped it.

Deliberately unlike Field Test 1: **there was no conversation.** Street ambience,
footsteps, wind, and the occasional *goedemorgen* to someone passing. Partway
through, Apple Fitness offered to record the walk as an outdoor exercise and the
offer was accepted, so a workout ran alongside the recording for the rest of it.

## The object

| Object | Captured by | Real audio | Manifest says | Place |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `20260728-0908 Recording` <br> `5e7a710f-53b0-4486-8597-6d4b8b9a33e6` | Apple Watch | 1156.4 s | 1156.4451700680272 s | Breda, Netherlands |

The source stays in the archive. Its manifest and `content.md`, the loudness
measurement, and the recognition runs are preserved under
[`evidence/`](2026-07-28-field-test-2/evidence).

## What held

Nearly everything, and several of these are yesterday's failures no longer
happening.

- **The Watch recorded for nineteen minutes in the background.** The app was left
  and other things were done; the watchOS microphone indicator stayed visible
  throughout. No `WKBackgroundModes` is declared, and none was needed.
- **A workout running alongside did not disturb the recording.** Fitness detected
  the walk, the exercise was accepted and recorded, and the microphone stayed
  live. The two coexisted for the rest of the walk.
- **When capture did end, the app said so.** A notification arrived, the object
  was saved in the background, and it stopped cleanly. This is the single thing
  Milestone 1.6 exists for, and it is the direct answer to Field Test 1's worst
  finding: yesterday twenty-five minutes vanished silently; today the same class
  of event announced itself and lost nothing.
- **The manifest records the true duration.** 1156.445 s, measured from the
  preserved file. Yesterday's equivalent said `0`.
- **The place name resolved.** `Breda, Netherlands`, not an empty string and not
  raw coordinates.
- **The language choice persisted.** The iPhone still had Dutch selected from the
  previous session rather than resetting to the system language.

## Findings

### 1. Recognition returned nothing, and the app called it a failure

Transcribing the object in Dutch failed on iPhone and again on the Mac. Both
showed the same thing: a badge reading **Needs attention**.

Nothing about the object needs attention. The run completed, read all 1156
seconds, and recognised no speech. The recording is intact and unchanged. Being
told an object needs attention when what happened is "we listened and heard
nothing" is precisely the class of dishonesty this milestone is named for — and
it is worse than saying nothing, because it points at the object rather than at
the situation.

**Evidence.** The recognition path was run directly over the source, one variable
changed, with [`recognise.swift`](2026-07-28-field-test-2/evidence/recognise.swift);
full output in
[`recognition-by-language.txt`](2026-07-28-field-test-2/evidence/recognition-by-language.txt).

| Object | Language | Module | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| `20260728-0908` | `nl-NL` | `DictationTranscriber` | **0** |
| `20260728-0908` | `en-US` | `SpeechTranscriber` | 6 — *"Yeah. Good Martha. Good morning. Hey."* |
| `20260727-0802` (control) | `nl-NL` | `DictationTranscriber` | 535 |

The control matters: the same code, the same language and the same device
produced 535 words from yesterday's walk, so nothing in the pipeline is broken.
The model was installed and reserved in every run.

**The recording really is mostly ambience**, which the English run's six
fragments — three of them a mangled *goedemorgen* — corroborate rather than
contradict. Measured with
[`loudness.py`](2026-07-28-field-test-2/evidence/loudness.py):

| | ambient floor | median | above floor +8 dB | sustained runs ≥ 2 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `20260728-0908` (this walk) | −47.6 dBFS | −43.5 dBFS | 23% | 146 s |
| `20260727-0802` (talking) | −44.3 dBFS | −37.0 dBFS | 45% | 326 s |

So "no speech recognised" is a defensible thing for the recogniser to conclude.
The failure is entirely in what was done with that conclusion.

**Root cause.** `OnbiiTranscriptionRun` throws `RunError.noSpeechRecognized` when
no track produced a segment. Both view models map *any* thrown error to
`OnbiiObjectActivity.failed`, which `OnbiiStatusIndicator` renders as
`needsAttention`. `OnbiiStatusBadge` then displays `shortTitle` — "Needs
attention" — and the actual sentence survives only in the accessibility label and
in a transient status line elsewhere in the window.

**What it asks of the implementation.** Three things, and they are separable:

- An attempt that completes and finds nothing is an **outcome**, not an error.
  The distinction is not cosmetic: one says the object is fine and the situation
  was quiet, the other says something is broken.
- The outcome should be **recorded on the object**, not held in a view model. As
  it stands, the object says `_Transcription pending._` and remembers nothing —
  not that Dutch was tried, not when, not that it found nothing. Someone opening
  it next week has no way to know it was ever attempted. Provenance is where
  that belongs; `0033` already requires a derived result to record what
  determined it, and an empty result is still a result.
- **The message has to reach the person.** A badge that can only say "Needs
  attention" cannot carry a reason, and the reason is the whole value.

### 2. It is the module, not the language — and level matters more than cleaning

The first reading of the table above was that Dutch is the problem, because
Dutch is the only variable that visibly changed. That reading was wrong, and it
took one more run to show it: **the same file, the same English, through the
other module.**

| audio | mean | language | module | words | text |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| original | −38.0 dBFS | `nl-NL` | Dictation | 0 | |
| original | −38.0 dBFS | `en-US` | **Speech** | 6 | *Yeah. Good Martha. Good morning. Hey.* |
| original | −38.0 dBFS | `en-US` | **Dictation** | **0** | |
| loudnorm | −19.5 dBFS | `nl-NL` | Dictation | 2 | *Ga weg* |
| loudnorm | −19.5 dBFS | `en-US` | Speech | 5 | *Hey, Martha. Good morning. Hey.* |
| +denoise | −22.5 dBFS | `nl-NL` | Dictation | 0 | |
| +denoise | −22.5 dBFS | `en-US` | Speech | 3 | *Good morning. Hey.* |
| 16 kHz speech band | −23.7 dBFS | `nl-NL` | Dictation | **1** | ***Goedemorgen*** |

Full runs and the exact `ffmpeg` filters in
[`recognition-treatments.txt`](2026-07-28-field-test-2/evidence/recognition-treatments.txt).

**Read the third row first.** English through `DictationTranscriber` finds
nothing, on the file where English through `SpeechTranscriber` finds six words.
The language was never the variable. `DictationTranscriber` simply needs far more
signal before it emits anything at all — which is consistent with the control:
give it two people talking clearly and it produces 535 words quite happily.

That is a worse problem than a language gap, because it is silent. A model that
degrades gracefully returns a poor transcript you can see is poor. This one
returns nothing, which is indistinguishable from *there was nothing there* — and
Dutch is on that model permanently, so a Dutch speaker gets the silent failure
mode and an English speaker does not.

**Level does help, and it is the cheapest thing that does.** Normalising the
level alone took the Dutch path from 0 to 2 words. The 16 kHz speech-band
treatment produced the single best result of any run: the actual word
*Goedemorgen*, correctly, in Dutch — something neither the untreated audio nor
the English path ever managed.

**But treatment is not monotonic, and this is the load-bearing caution.** Every
treatment made the *working* module worse: 6 → 5 with normalisation, 6 → 3 with
denoising. And the denoiser took the Dutch path back to 0, undoing what
normalisation had gained. Whatever the denoiser removed, the recogniser was
using.

So "boost, clean and filter before recognising" is a real direction with real
evidence behind it — and it cannot be a fixed pipeline applied to everything. The
treatment that rescues one recogniser damages another.

**On the size of this sample.** There are about six recognisable words in
nineteen minutes. Every number in that table is a single-digit count, and
single-digit counts are directional at best. What the table establishes is not
how much each treatment is worth; it is that the effect exists, has a sign, and
is measurable on a real object — and how to measure it on the next one.

This belongs to the thread
[Capture Situations And Processing](../architecture/capture-situations-and-processing.md)
opened, not to any milestone. It also reframes Field Test 1's fourth finding:
some of what was read as "the situation is too noisy" is a recogniser that stops
emitting below a threshold, and the two are separable by exactly the run above.

The known-gap note in
[Milestone 1.5](../milestones/milestone-1.5.md#known-milestone-gaps) — "Dutch
works, but through the older dictation model" — was right about the mechanism and
understated the consequence.

### 3. The Mac reads the archive once, and then never again

The object was created on the walk and reached iCloud Drive. The Mac app was
started afterwards and its list did not contain it. Finder showed the object in
the archive folder; **Refresh Archive** in the app then showed it too.

**Root cause**, found in the code rather than inferred: `reloadObjects()` is
called from `ImportViewModel.init()`, from `ContentView`'s `.task`, from the
Refresh Archive menu item, and after an import. Nothing else. There is no
directory watcher and no `NSMetadataQuery`, so an object that arrives while the
window is open — by sync, by Finder, by any other route — is invisible until
someone asks again. The iPhone re-reads on `scenePhase` becoming active; the Mac
has no equivalent.

Whether iCloud had actually delivered the object at the moment the app launched
is not knowable now, and does not change the conclusion: the app presented a list
as *the archive* while having no way to know whether it still was.

`OnbiiArchiveIndex`'s own doc comment says "the filesystem is the truth every
time it is asked". That is true and it is the right design. The gap is that
nothing asks.

## What this settles

**Spike A is closed, and the answer is not the one the spike was designed to
find.** The question was how long a Watch recording survives losing the
foreground. It survives indefinitely as far as this test goes — nineteen minutes
across app switches and a workout starting, with nothing declared. What ends a
recording is not runtime, it is **another app taking the audio session**.

That is a different problem from the one Field Test 1 hypothesised, and a much
better understood one. `WKBackgroundModes` stays undeclared;
[Watch Capture Modes](../architecture/watch-capture-modes.md) stays optional
rather than urgent.

It also retires the last of Field Test 1's mistaken diagnosis. Yesterday's
1200.098 s then silence was read as watchOS suspending an app with no runtime
claim. Today the same app recorded through everything watchOS could throw at it
and stopped the moment another app spoke. The Workout app was the cause, as the
correction to that record already suspected.

## Unanswered

### A decision, for the person whose knowledge this is

**Should a foreign interruption end the recording, or pause it?**

Today's behaviour — preserve what was captured, stop, and say so — is honest, and
it is a large improvement on vanishing. But it interacts badly with the primary
use case. Fitness announces a split roughly every kilometre. This walk got one
announcement and one stop; a longer walk-and-talk would be cut at the first
kilometre and again at every kilometre after, which is exactly the "long solo
Watch recording" that
[Milestone 1.6](../milestones/milestone-1.6.md#the-use-case-that-raises-the-stakes)
names as primary.

The options are genuinely different in kind, not in degree:

- **Stop, as now.** Honest, simple, and produces one object per kilometre.
- **Resume into the same file.** `AVAudioRecorder` does not resume by itself, and
  the audio during the announcement is gone either way, so this appends across a
  seam that nothing declares. It makes a discontinuous recording look continuous
  and silently shifts every timestamp after the seam. Not available: it is the
  source telling a lie about itself.
- **Resume into a new source *in the same object*.** The walk stays one object;
  each stretch of audio is its own preserved `source` resource with its own
  `captureStartedAt`. The gap is declared by construction — it is the space
  between one resource ending and the next beginning — and nothing is mixed,
  appended or invented.
- **Ask the person to turn the announcements off** before a reflection walk.
  Costs nothing to build, costs the person something to remember, and does
  nothing about a phone call.

**The recommendation is the third**, and the reason is that Onbii is already
shaped for it rather than that it is the nicest idea. Dual-source call capture
already puts two `source` resources in one object;
`OnbiiTranscriptionRun` already transcribes every audio source and merges the
tracks onto one timeline using each resource's `captureStartedAt` offset;
`OnbiiImportRequest` already takes an array of sources. A walk interrupted three
times is the same shape as a call with two legs. What is missing is the capture
side: noticing that an interruption ended, reclaiming the session, and starting
the next segment.

It also fits the principles rather than straining against them. Every byte
captured is still preserved as an original, nothing is mixed, and the
discontinuity is a recorded fact instead of a silence someone has to infer.

**It should not hold up Milestone 1.6.** What ships today — preserve, stop, say
so — is honest and is a large improvement on vanishing. Segmented continuation is
capture work on three platforms plus a field test of its own, and it wants to be
built deliberately rather than bolted on to close a milestone.

**Decided, 28 July.** Segmented continuation is the direction, with the caveat
recorded rather than smoothed over: it is the best available handling and not a
good outcome. The audio spoken over is gone, and no amount of careful
segmentation recovers it. It earns its place by working for interruptions nothing
can anticipate — a call, Siri, an alarm.

For the one interruption that *can* be anticipated, the answer is to remove the
cause rather than survive it: a reflection-walk mode where Onbii owns the workout
session, so the Watch never offers to record a walk and Fitness never speaks over
the recording. That was already on the backlog as
[Watch Capture Modes](../architecture/watch-capture-modes.md), written for a
runtime problem that turned out not to exist; this test gave it a better reason.

Both are gathered into [Milestone 1.7](../milestones/milestone-1.7.md), which is
captured and not scoped. Milestone 1.5's always-ready strand resumes first.

### Empirical, for the next test

- Does the recording survive a whole walk with Fitness's spoken announcements
  and audio feedback disabled? That isolates the announcement from the workout,
  which this test could not.
- Does a second recording started immediately after an interruption run
  normally, or does the workout keep claiming the session? One walk, one
  deliberate restart.
- How many words does yesterday's 08:02 walk produce through `SpeechTranscriber`
  in English versus `DictationTranscriber` in Dutch, given it is Dutch speech?
  Not a fair comparison of accuracy, but it bounds how much of Field Test 1's
  missing speech is the model rather than the situation.
