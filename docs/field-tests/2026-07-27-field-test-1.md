# Field Test 1 — 27 July 2026

The first use of Onbii away from a desk: a morning walk through Breda, then a
conversation at a table. Three objects were produced, two on Apple Watch Ultra 2
and one on iPhone Air. Everything below came out of that hour and none of it had
surfaced in development.

Milestone 1 was declared complete two days earlier, on validated devices. It was
complete in the sense that mattered then — the capture-certainty loop worked. It
was not ready for a walk.

## Conditions

A city centre: pavement, a park, a main road. Constant traffic and wind, two
people talking while moving, a Watch on the wrist and later an iPhone flat on a
table between two people. The phone's system language was Australian English for
the first recording and Dutch for the rest; the conversation was Dutch
throughout.

None of this is exotic. It is the ordinary case.

## The objects

The sources stay in the archive, which is their home — this record references
them by stable object ID rather than copying them, and the manifests, both
Markdown renderings and the word-level derived transcript of each are preserved
under [`evidence/`](2026-07-27-field-test-1/evidence) so every claim here can be
checked without the audio.

| Object | Captured by | Real audio | Manifest says | Locale used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `20260727-0802 Recording` <br> `71b7116c-d919-42c6-beab-46419dcd794d` | Apple Watch | 1200.1 s | **0 s** | `en-AU` |
| `20260727-0857 Recording` <br> `702e4ce8-1989-43e6-ae47-651e38328558` | Apple Watch | 143.4 s | 143.4 s | `nl-NL` |
| `20260727-0902 Recording` <br> `d882dedc-3e4b-47a2-a709-973d1e0de559` | iPhone | 44.3 s | 44.1 s | `nl-NL` |

## What held

Worth stating first, because it is the part that was designed for and it worked.

- **Every byte that was captured was preserved.** All three `.m4a` files are
  intact, complete for the audio that reached them, and independently readable.
  No failure below is a lost source.
- **The recordings survived a hostile morning** — an app suspension on the Watch
  and a device lock on the iPhone — and still reached the archive as well-formed
  objects the reader accepts, including the one whose capture was cut short.
- **The archive was inspectable when the app was not.** Every finding here was
  diagnosed from the objects on disk, with no logs, no debugger, and no access
  to the devices. That is the local-first principle earning its keep.
- **Provenance told the story.** The `en-AU` locale of the first recording is
  recorded in its derived transcript; the language mistake was recoverable as a
  fact rather than a memory.

## Findings

### 1. A recording can die without the app noticing

The most serious finding, and the reason to run field tests at all.

The 08:02 Watch recording contains **exactly 1200.098 s** of audio. It started at
08:02:37 and stopped at 08:22:37. It was stopped by hand at 08:47 — so roughly
25 minutes of the walk was never recorded. During that time the Watch showed
"Recording is visibly active".

What happened, in order:

1. watchOS offered to record an outdoor walk. Attention moved to the Workout app.
2. The Watch app declares no `WKBackgroundModes` and never takes a
   `WKExtendedRuntimeSession` — neither appears anywhere in the repository. It
   has no sanctioned claim on runtime once it stops being frontmost, so watchOS
   was entitled to suspend it, and the `AVAudioRecorder` stopped.
3. Nothing observed that. `OnbiiMicrophoneRecorder` sets no
   `AVAudioRecorderDelegate` and observes no `AVAudioSession.interruptionNotification`;
   neither exists in the codebase. The UI went on claiming to record.
4. The displayed timer read wrong because `duration` returns
   `audioRecorder?.currentTime`, which is documented to be `0` when the recorder
   is not running.
5. On stop, that same `0` was written to the manifest as `durationSeconds`, and
   the writer accepted it. A twenty-minute source is filed as zero seconds.

Whether 1200.098 s is a hard watchOS ceiling or simply when the Workout app took
over cannot be determined from the file. It does not change any of the fixes.

**The same class of failure on iPhone.** `Apps/OnbiiIOS/Info.plist` declares no
`UIBackgroundModes` at all. The 09:02 recording is 44 s long because the phone
auto-locked and iOS suspended the app. Again silently.

What this asks of the implementation:

- A capture session must hold an explicit, declared claim on background runtime
  on both platforms, or must refuse to start and say so.
- Interruption and unexpected-stop must be observed and surfaced. An app that
  cannot honestly report "I am recording" should not display it. Settled under
  [the app must say when capture or transfer fails](#the-app-must-say-when-capture-or-transfer-fails)
  below.
- The writer must re-derive duration from the file it just preserved. It is the
  only number it can actually verify, and taking the capture side's word for it
  put a falsehood in a manifest.

The last point is a format-integrity issue, not a UI one: a manifest is supposed
to describe the preserved source truthfully.

### 2. A place name that failed produced an empty label, not an absent one

All three manifests carry good coordinates and `"name": ""`.

That empty string is the finding. Nothing in `Codable` turns `nil` into `""`, so
the reverse geocode *succeeded* and returned an empty label — and the recordings
from 25 and 26 July, through the identical code path, carry
`"name": "Breda, Netherlands"`. The geocoder works. These particular
coordinates, a few hundred metres north in the park, resolve to a map item with
no city label.

Two places then failed to treat empty as absent:

- `OnbiiLocationProvider.placeName` chains fallbacks with `??`, so an empty
  string from the first choice short-circuits `cityName`, `shortAddress` and
  `item.name` — the fallbacks never ran.
- Both detail views render `location.name ?? coordinates(…)`, so `""` displays as
  a blank row. `OnbiiContentMarkdown` already guards with `!name.isEmpty`, which
  is why `content.md` shows `Location: 51.5818, 4.7770` while the app showed
  nothing.

The general lesson is worth more than the fix: **for a best-effort field, absent
and empty must be the same thing**, and the check belongs where the value is
produced, not at each of the places that read it.

### 3. Language was chosen silently, and the choice cannot be revisited

The 08:02 recording was transcribed as Australian English because that was the
phone's system language. The result is unusable — Dutch speech rendered as
English near-homophones.

- The language is never chosen by a person. `selectedLanguageID` is ordinary
  view state, not persisted, so every launch resets it to the system language.
- **The object cannot be transcribed again.** `canTranscribe` is
  `hasTranscribableAudio && !hasTranscript` on both platforms, so once any
  transcript exists the action disappears.
- The locale used is recorded only in `derived/transcript.json`. The manifest
  does not carry it and `transcript.md` does not state it.

The first two together mean a single silent wrong guess permanently degrades an
object whose source is perfectly good. That directly contradicts the principle
that derived data is not truth and can always be regenerated: here it cannot.

Re-transcription is mechanically straightforward — the enricher already supports
replacing artifacts — but it is a **supersede**, not an overwrite. A second
transcript should arrive with its own provenance, recording which language and
which model produced it, and the first should not silently vanish. That was the
design decision this finding really surfaced; it is settled under
[reprocessing supersedes](#reprocessing-supersedes-and-nothing-is-thrown-away)
below.

The ambition beyond it — recognising the language automatically, or handling
mixed Dutch and English within one conversation — is a real requirement for this
user, and this conversation was itself the mixed case. Choosing correctly is
step one.

### 4. Most audible speech produced no words

Measured with [`evidence/coverage.py`](2026-07-27-field-test-1/evidence/coverage.py),
comparing per-window loudness against the word timings in each derived
transcript:

| Object | Ambient floor | Speech-level audio | Of that, no words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 08:02 Watch, walking | −44.3 dBFS | 45% of file | **71%** (382 s) |
| 08:57 Watch, walking | −42.3 dBFS | 56% | **54%** (44 s) |
| 09:02 iPhone, on table | −31.3 dBFS | 60% | **66%** (18 s) |

No file contains any silence of 1.5 s or longer below −40 dB. There is a
continuous ambient bed throughout all three — as expected beside a main road.

The walking figures overstate lost speech, because outdoors "speech-level"
also catches wind and traffic. **The 09:02 figure is the trustworthy one**: a
quiet table, the correct language, and a transcript that simply stops at 00:25
of a 44 s recording while audible conversation continues. A second person was
present in all three recordings; only the 08:02 transcript attributes anything
to a second speaker, and the other two put every recognised word on one.

So the audio is not the problem — recognition is dropping it. Contributing
factors to test, in rough order of expected effect:

- The iOS session uses `.playAndRecord` with `.defaultToSpeaker`, which biases
  the input chain toward speakerphone processing rather than faithful room
  capture. `.record` with `.measurement` mode is the first thing to try.
- A single processed mono stream is all `AVAudioRecorder` yields. The iPhone's
  multiple microphones give no separation or beamforming unless explicitly
  asked for; expecting the hardware to help by itself was wrong.
- 44.1 kHz mono AAC at ~63 kbps, later downsampled to 16 kHz for both
  recognition and speaker embedding. Worth confirming the capture rate is not
  costing anything on the way.

This needs a tuning loop against these three objects as fixtures, not a guess.

### 5. A sentence splits mid-phrase into a speaker called "Recording"

In the transcripts, one speaker's sentence breaks in two and the second half is
attributed to "Recording":

```
**00:08 Speaker 1:** Ik heb nu m'n telefoon in het
**00:11 Recording:** Nederlands gezet
```

Two bugs stacked, and both are small.

`OnbiiSpeakerWindowing.windows` can emit a window shorter than the embedder's
one-second minimum: an isolated short word bounded by pauses longer than
`maxGapSeconds` becomes a window of just that word. "Nu" is 0.18 s. The Core ML
embedder returns `nil` for it and `speakerID` stays unset — 7, 9 and 4 words
across the three objects.

`OnbiiTranscriptMarkdown` then renders an unassigned word under its *capture
track* label, which for a single-track recording reads "Recording". A track name
that looks like a person's name is the actual harm: the fallback was honest
source attribution when nothing had been diarized, but inside a diarized
transcript it reads as a second speaker and severs a sentence.

Both directions are clear. The windower should widen the *audio* range around a
short window rather than give up — the embedder holds the whole track and can
read context either side of the word. The renderer should attach unassigned
segments to the neighbouring turn whenever the document is diarized at all, and
reserve track labels for transcripts with no diarization.

## What field testing bought

Every finding above is a condition that does not occur at a desk: another app
competing for the foreground, a screen that locks itself, a place with no city
label, a language set for something else, and a road full of traffic. The two
that matter most — silent capture death, and an object degraded permanently by a
guess — are both failures of *honesty* rather than capability. The app claimed
things that were not true, and the manifest recorded one of them.

That is the argument for doing this regularly, and for treating these three
objects as fixtures rather than anecdotes.

## What the findings settled

Decided on 27 July, directly out of this test. Where these change the shared
format they need decision records in the
[spec's decision log](../spec/docs/decisions); that is noted per item.

### Reprocessing supersedes, and nothing is thrown away

The first real case of reprocessing, raised by the object that was transcribed
in the wrong language and could not be transcribed again.

**Now:** reprocessing is a deliberate act, never automatic. The newest result
becomes the current one — it is what `content.md` and the preview show — and
earlier results are retained rather than overwritten, so it is possible to see
what changed. Retention is affordable: these are text files. Compressing older
versions is a later optimisation, not a reason to discard them.

**Later:** a person chooses which version is current, and derived work built on
a transcript — summaries, tasks — records which version it was derived from, so
drift from a newer transcript is visible rather than silent.

This is the shape of versioning generally, not a transcript special case. It
needs a spec decision record, and it is the first thing that will exercise the
existing "human edits are first-class" and "derived data is not truth"
principles against a real object.

### An object records how its transcript was produced

The language and the model belong in the manifest, not only in
`derived/transcript.json`. Without that, "this transcript is wrong" cannot be
distinguished from "this transcript was made under the wrong assumption", which
is exactly what happened here. Versioned along with the transcript itself, per
the decision above. Also a spec matter.

### The app must say when capture or transfer fails

Prevention first — declared background runtime, so a recording is not
interrupted in the first place. But interruptions will still happen, and the
system must tell the person when one does rather than continue displaying a
state that is not true.

This applies to every stage of the pipeline, not just the microphone: a Watch
recording that has not reached the iPhone is equally a silent failure today.
Onbii currently sends no notifications at all, so this is new surface rather
than a fix, and an Apple implementation matter rather than a spec one.

### Automatic language detection is an implementation matter

How a language gets detected is platform-dependent — Apple, and one day
Windows, Linux, Android or Sailfish, will each have different capabilities, and
the choice of technique should not be frozen into the shared format. Apple's
stack appears to be further along here than most, though that is an assumption
worth checking rather than a finding.

What the **spec** needs to allow is the *record*: that detection was automatic
rather than chosen, and which language or languages were identified and used —
including more than one within a single object, since conversations that run
Dutch and English together are the ordinary case for this user.

## Addendum — 27 July, evening: the first object was transcribed again

Later the same day, with the first fixes in place, `71b7116c` — the 08:02 walk —
was transcribed again, in Dutch this time. It is the first real exercise of
reprocessing, and it separates two things that had been tangled together.

Measured with
[`evidence/compare-generations.py`](2026-07-27-field-test-1/evidence/compare-generations.py),
against the same source audio for both generations. The object now holds both,
so this can be re-run:

| | en-AU, 06:52 | nl-NL, 12:58 |
|---|---|---|
| Words recognised | 372 | **531** |
| Speech-level audio with no words | 71% (382 s) | **58% (308 s)** |
| Speaker distribution | 54 / 311 | 226 / 298 / 7 |
| Words the embedder could not place | 7 | **0** |

**Reprocessing works, and the object is legible over time.** The English
generation is retained under `superseded/20260727T125853Z/`, laid out as the
object used to be, with a `superseded` provenance event naming which result
replaced which. The manifest records that the newer one was made in `nl-NL` and
that the language was chosen rather than detected. The 09:02 recording ran
through the same path. Nothing about
[the earlier finding](#3-language-was-chosen-silently-and-the-choice-cannot-be-revisited)
survives except its evidence, which is the point.

**Two of the fixes are visible in the numbers.** The seven words that had no
speaker are now zero: widening the embedder's audio window around an isolated
short word placed all of them. And a lopsided 54/311 split became 226/298, which
is what two people talking on a walk should look like — the English model had
been recognising almost nothing from one of them. A third speaker appears with
seven words; too small to trust either way, and worth watching rather than
concluding anything from.

**Correcting the language was necessary and nowhere near sufficient.** It
recovered 43% more words and cut unrecognised speech-level audio by 74 seconds.
It is still missing 58% of it. Read as Dutch by a Dutch speaker, the result is
not usable — not wrong word by word, but too sparse to follow.

The shape of the loss is worth recording, because it points somewhere specific.
Recognition runs the whole file: the last word lands at 1193 s of 1200 s, as it
did in English. Where words do appear they appear at a normal conversational
rate, roughly 2.3 per second across the 227 seconds it covered. So this is not
uniform degradation — the recogniser works, then drops long stretches, then
works again.

That makes it
[finding 4](#4-most-audible-speech-produced-no-words) exactly, unchanged and
untouched by any of this: audio that was captured but not recognised. It also
means the language was never the whole story, and an object being permanently
degraded was hiding a second problem behind it.

The remaining question needs a walk rather than a desk. This recording was cut
short at 1200 s by the app being suspended, so it also cannot answer whether
1200 s is a ceiling. Both belong to the next field test, and to the tuning loop.

**What this did not fix.** The manifest still says `durationSeconds: 0` and
`"name": ""`. The writer measures durations for objects written from now on, and
the geocoder no longer stamps an empty name, but neither reaches back to an
object already on disk — and reprocessing regenerated `content.md` from a
manifest that still carries both. Repairing a recorded fact is a different
operation from superseding a derived result: it needs its own provenance, and it
is not built.

## Still to test

Empirical questions this test raised and could not answer. They need a
deliberate repeat, not a decision.

- Does watchOS grant background audio recording to a third-party app that
  declares a background mode, or does capture on the Watch require an extended
  runtime session — or a different approach altogether?
- Is 1200 s a real ceiling, or simply when the Workout app took the foreground?
  Repeat the walk without touching the workout prompt and see where it stops.
- Does `.record` with `.measurement` mode recover the speech that recognition is
  currently dropping? This one splits in two, and the split matters: the
  *recognition* side — locale form, which on-device model, audio
  pre-normalisation — can be tested against these three objects as fixtures,
  one variable at a time. The *capture* side cannot, because changing the audio
  session changes what gets recorded. That half needs a fresh A/B recording:
  same room, same people, same script, back to back.
- Where does recognition drop a stretch? The addendum above shows it running the
  whole file and losing long runs rather than degrading evenly, which is a
  different question from "is the model good enough" and probably a more
  answerable one.
